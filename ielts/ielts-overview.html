IELTS General Training — Overview (for the teacher)

This is a reference for you, the teacher. The student does not need to read all of this now. In this course the student meets IELTS gradually (see the last section). Use this page to understand the test you are preparing them for.

1. What is IELTS?

IELTS (International English Language Testing System) is the world's most widely used English test for migration, work, and study. It is co-owned by the British Council, IDP: IELTS Australia, and Cambridge English. It measures English across four skills — Listening, Reading, Writing, and Speaking — and reports results on a 9-band scale (see Section 4).

There is no "pass" or "fail." You receive a band score, and the organisation you apply to (an employer, a province, or the federal immigration authority) decides what band they require.

2. General Training vs Academic

IELTS comes in two versions. The Listening and Speaking sections are identical in both. Only the Reading and Writing sections differ.

Aspect General Training Academic
Purpose Work, migration, secondary school, training programs University/college study, professional registration (e.g. doctors, nurses)
Listening Same as Academic Same as GT
Speaking Same as Academic Same as GT
Reading Everyday texts: notices, ads, timetables, workplace documents, one longer general-interest passage Three long academic articles (journals, textbooks)
Writing Task 1 Write a letter (~150 words) Describe a chart/graph/diagram (~150 words)
Writing Task 2 Essay (~250 words) Essay (~250 words) — more academic register

Why General Training is the right choice here

This student needs IELTS for work/immigration, not to study at a university. For most work and skilled-immigration programs:

  • Most work and skilled-immigration programs accept IELTS General Training.
  • IELTS Academic is only needed for university study or for certain regulated professions that demand it.

General Training is also generally more approachable for a beginner: the Reading texts are everyday materials (signs, emails, instructions) rather than dense academic articles, and the Writing Task 1 is a practical letter rather than data description.

Important: For work/immigration purposes the student should take IELTS General Training, not the IELTS Life Skills test or "IELTS for UKVI" unless a specific program says otherwise. Confirm the exact test name the chosen program demands before booking.

3. The four sections

Total test time is about 2 hours 45 minutes for Listening + Reading + Writing (done in one sitting, no breaks). Speaking may be on the same day or up to 7 days before or after.

Listening (~30 minutes + transfer time on paper)

  • 4 recordings, played once only.
  • 40 questions total.
  • The recordings move from everyday to more formal/academic in feel:
    1. A conversation between two people in an everyday social context.
    2. A monologue in an everyday social context (e.g. a speech about local facilities).
    3. A conversation among up to four people in a training/education context.
    4. A monologue on an academic subject.
  • Uses a range of native-speaker accents — British, Australian, New Zealand, American.
  • On paper, candidates get 10 extra minutes at the end to transfer answers to the answer sheet. On computer, there is no separate transfer time (about 2 minutes to review).
  • Same for both GT and Academic.

Reading (60 minutes)

  • 40 questions, no extra transfer time (answers written/typed as you go).
  • The General Training Reading uses real-world, everyday materials and gets progressively longer/harder:
    1. Section 1 — "social survival": short everyday texts such as notices, advertisements, timetables.
    2. Section 2 — "workplace survival": work-related texts such as job descriptions, contracts, staff handbooks, training materials.
    3. Section 3 — "general reading": one longer, more complex piece of continuous prose on a general-interest topic.
  • This is easier than Academic Reading, which uses three dense academic passages. Good news for a beginner — but the time pressure (40 questions in 60 minutes) is real and must be trained.

Writing (60 minutes)

Two tasks. Task 2 is worth more of the Writing score, so manage time accordingly (suggest ~20 min on Task 1, ~40 min on Task 2).

Task What it is Length Notes
Task 1 Write a letter in response to a situation ~150 words Can be formal, semi-formal, or informal depending on the prompt (e.g. a complaint to a company = formal; a note to a friend = informal). Register matters.
Task 2 Write an essay responding to a point of view, argument, or problem ~250 words More general/personal topics than Academic. Clear structure, examples, opinion.

Writing below the word count is penalised, so the student must learn to hit the minimums.

Speaking (11–14 minutes)

  • A face-to-face (or video call) interview with a real examiner — not a computer.
  • Recorded for quality/monitoring. Three parts:
    1. Part 1 — Introduction & interview (4–5 min): identity check, then everyday questions about home, work, family, interests.
    2. Part 2 — Long turn (3–4 min): the candidate gets a task card, 1 minute to prepare, then speaks for 1–2 minutes on the topic, followed by a couple of rounded-off questions.
    3. Part 3 — Discussion (4–5 min): a two-way discussion of more abstract ideas connected to the Part 2 topic.
  • Same for both GT and Academic.

4. The 9-band scale

Each of the four sections gets a band from 1 to 9 (including half-bands like 6.5). The four are averaged into an Overall Band Score (rounded to the nearest half band).

Band Label Rough meaning
9 Expert Fully operational command; native-like.
8 Very good Fully operational with occasional slips.
7 Good Operational command; handles complex language.
6 Competent Generally effective despite some errors.
5 Modest Partial command; copes with overall meaning.
4 Limited Basic competence in familiar situations only.
3 Extremely limited Conveys/understands only general meaning.
2 Intermittent Very limited; great difficulty.
1 Non-user No real ability.
0 Did not attempt No assessable information.

An absolute beginner today would test around Band 1–3. Reaching Band 5–7 by late 2026 is the kind of climb this course is built around.

Choosing a target band

The 9-band scale above is what matters for planning. A realistic climb for this course is into the Band 5–7 range by late 2026.

Confirm before targeting a score: the exact band the student needs depends on their specific work or immigration program — different programs and job requirements set different minimum scores. Check the program's stated English requirement before locking a goal score.

5. Test-day logistics (high level)

  • ID: The student must bring the same valid passport they used to register. No passport on the day = no test. (Photo is taken at the centre.)
  • Paper vs computer-delivered: IELTS is offered in two delivery formats — paper-based and computer-delivered. The content, format, scoring, and difficulty are identical; only the input method differs.
    • Computer-delivered: type Writing, click answers; results usually in 3–5 days; far more test dates available.
    • Paper-based: handwrite everything; results in about 13 days.
    • Speaking is the same for both (live examiner).
  • Results: A Test Report Form (TRF) shows the four section bands plus the overall band. Scores are valid for 2 years.
  • Decide format with the student later — for a beginner, computer-delivered often suits if their typing is reasonable and they want faster results; paper suits those more comfortable writing by hand.

6. How IELTS fits into THIS course

  • Stage 1 is light familiarization only. At this stage the student is an absolute beginner; the focus is foundational English (sounds, basic vocabulary, simple sentences, everyday survival language). IELTS is mentioned only so the goal feels real — no real test practice yet.
  • Real IELTS practice begins in Stage 2–3, once the student has enough English to attempt simplified versions of the task types and gradually build up to full timed sections.
  • Speaking is coached live by the teacher. Because IELTS Speaking is a live interview with a real examiner, the student rehearses it directly with you — mock Part 1/2/3 interviews, feedback on fluency, pronunciation, and answer structure — rather than through any automated tool.

Keep this document for your own reference and reveal pieces of it to the student as they become ready.
