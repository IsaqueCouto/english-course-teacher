Lesson 8 — Reading Signs and Notices

How to teach this

This is the student's first real taste of the IELTS reading test — gentle and low-pressure. In IELTS General Training, the Reading test starts with what is called Section 1 ("social survival"): short, everyday texts like notices, signs, advertisements, and timetables. (See ielts/ielts-overview.md for the full picture — you do not need to show the student all of it.) These are exactly the kinds of texts they will read every day after moving abroad: a shop window, a door, a workplace memo, a sign in a building.

The skill we are building is reading for meaning, fast — not understanding every single word. Teach the student to:

  • Skim first. Look at the whole sign quickly. What is it about? A shop? A door? A machine?
  • Guess from context. If a word is new, the rest of the sign and the place usually give it away. "OUT OF ORDER" on a machine means the machine is broken — they can guess this even without knowing "order."
  • Not panic over unknown words. This is the single most important habit. In real life and in IELTS, nobody knows every word. Reassure the student constantly: "You don't need every word. You need the meaning."

Keep the mood light. This is a first taste, not a test. Praise good guessing even when the guess is wrong — the strategy is what we are training. Everything here is reading and writing only; the student does not speak any of it. Let them point, circle, match, and write their answers on the sheet.

A note on a new task type: this lesson introduces True / False / Not Given, a classic IELTS reading question type. Explain it carefully (the student sheet has a simple explanation and example) because the "Not Given" idea is genuinely tricky and new. The key teaching point: Not Given means the text does not tell us — it is not the same as False. False means the text says the opposite.

Objective

By the end of this lesson the student can read everyday signs and short notices and answer comprehension questions about them — including the IELTS True / False / Not Given question type. This is the core IELTS GT Reading Section 1 skill, met for the first time in a gentle, scaffolded way.

Flow with timings (~45–50 min)

Warm-up (5 min). Show the student a few signs they already half-know from everyday life — for example, write OPEN and CLOSED on paper. Ask them (in writing or by pointing) which one means a shop is working. Then connect it to the goal: "Where you are moving, signs like these are everywhere — on doors, machines, walls. Today we learn to read them and answer questions. This is the first part of the IELTS reading test." Light PT gloss is fine here: "placas e avisos" = signs and notices.

Present (10 min). Introduce the idea of skimming and guessing from context (see "How to teach this"). Walk through one sign together on the board — for example:

NO PARKING

Ask: What is this about? (Cars.) Where would you see it? (A street, a driveway.) What does it tell you to do? (Do not leave your car here.) Model out the thinking so the student sees the strategy. Then introduce True / False / Not Given slowly, using the explanation on the student sheet. Do the example item together.

Practice (15 min). The student works through the signs and the matching task (Task A) and the "where would you see this?" task (Task C) on the sheet. The first item of each task is already done as an example. Let them work quietly in writing; you circulate and nudge. When they hit a new word, prompt: "Don't worry about that word — look at the whole sign. What place is this?"

Produce (10–12 min). The student reads the longer notice (the laundry-room rules) and answers the four written questions (Task D), plus the True / False / Not Given set (Task B). This is the closest thing to a real IELTS Section 1 item. Let them take their time — no clock pressure today. The goal is comfort and accuracy, not speed.

Wrap-up (5 min). Review answers together on paper. Celebrate correct guesses from context. Reinforce the big idea in writing: "You read signs and notices and found the meaning — even with new words. This is exactly what the IELTS reading test asks for." Hand out homework.

Common mistakes & gentle corrections

  • Confusing "False" and "Not Given." This is the most common and most natural error. Gently re-explain: False = the text says the opposite; Not Given = the text says nothing about it. Walk back through the example. Expect to repeat this more than once — it is normal.
  • Trying to understand every word. If the student freezes on one unknown word, cover it with a finger and ask them to read the rest. Show them the meaning is still clear. Praise the guess.
  • Reading the question before skimming the sign. Encourage skimming the sign first, then reading the question. (In later stages this order can flip for speed, but for now, meaning first.)
  • Over-thinking short signs. A sign like "STAFF ONLY" means just that — workers, not customers. If the student writes a long complicated answer, gently point back to the plain meaning.
  • Mixing up "Out of order" with "out of stock." A real false-friend trap. Out of order = broken / not working. Out of stock = none left to sell. Note it kindly if it comes up.

Homework

The student sheet's Homework section asks the student to:

  1. Find or remember three signs from real life (a shop, a door, a machine, a notice) and write each one out, then write one sentence explaining what it means and where you would see it.
  2. Re-read the laundry-room notice on the sheet and write two new True / False / Not Given statements of their own about it, with the answers.

Review this at the start of the next lesson. The "write your own statements" task is excellent diagnostic evidence of whether the True / False / Not Given concept has landed.
