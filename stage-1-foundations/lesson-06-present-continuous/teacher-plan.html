Lesson 6 — Present Continuous: Happening Now

How to teach this

You do all the talking. The student only reads, listens, and writes — never speaks. When an activity is a listening task, you read the sentence aloud yourself and the student writes what they hear or writes the answer. Keep the pace gentle: walk through the solved example in each exercise together before the student works alone. Use the Portuguese glosses on the student sheet only to confirm understanding, not as a translation drill. Praise correct use of "to be" + -ing, since this is the main hurdle for a Portuguese speaker.

Objective

By the end, the student can write present-continuous sentences (am/is/are + verb-ing) for actions happening now, and can tell the difference between the present simple (a routine, every day) and the present continuous (happening right now).

Target language

  • Structure: subject + am/is/are + verb-ing
    • I am working / He is working / They are working
  • -ing spelling rules:
    • Most verbs: just add -ing → work → working, eat → eating, read → reading
    • Verbs ending in -e: drop the e → write → writing, cook → cooking (note: cook keeps both o's; the e-drop rule is for verbs like write, make, take)
    • Short verbs ending consonant-vowel-consonant: double the last consonant → run → running, sit → sitting
  • Time markers: now, right now, at the moment, today
  • Contrast with present simple: "Every day I work" (routine) vs "I am working now" (now)
  • Action verbs to use: eat, read, work, write, study, watch, cook, walk, talk, wait

Flow with timings (~45–50 min)

Warm-up (~5 min) On paper or board, you write two sentences and read them aloud: "Every day I drink coffee." / "I am drinking coffee now." Ask the student to write which one means "now" and which means "every day". This shows the contrast before any rule.

Present (~10 min) Present the structure am/is/are + verb-ing. Write the pronoun table: I am, he/she/it is, you/we/they are. Then teach the three -ing spelling patterns with one clear example each (work → working, write → writing, run → running). Read each example aloud while the student follows on the sheet. Briefly point out the grammar trap: English needs "to be" — we say "She is working", not "She working". In Portuguese, "está trabalhando / está a trabalhar" already carries the "is", so it is easy to forget the English "is".

Practice (~20 min) Work through the student sheet exercises (a)–(d). For each, read the solved example aloud and confirm the student understands it before they continue alone. These are recognition and short-writing tasks: forming -ing words, building sentences from prompts, choosing simple vs continuous, and correcting errors. Circulate and give gentle corrections in writing.

Produce — student WRITES (~10 min) Exercises (e) and (f). For (e), read the short scene text aloud once while the student reads along, then they answer the questions in writing. For (f), the student writes 4–5 sentences about what people are doing right now, using the sentence starters provided. Remind them every sentence needs am/is/are.

Wrap-up (~5 min) You read three sentences aloud, mixing routine and now (e.g., "He reads the news every morning." / "He is reading the news now."). The student writes "every day" or "now" for each. Review the homework instructions.

Common mistakes & gentle corrections

  • Missing "to be": "She working now." → Gently show: "We need is: She is working now." (This is the Portuguese-speaker trap — "está" hides the "is".)
  • Wrong -ing spelling: "writeing" → "We drop the e: writing." / "runing" → "We double the n: running."
  • Wrong form of "be": "They is eating." → "With they we use are: They are eating."
  • Using present simple for "now": "He eats now." → "For something happening now, we use is eating: He is eating now."
  • Always correct in writing and let the student rewrite the corrected sentence, never asking them to speak it.

Homework

On the student sheet: complete the two written tasks — (1) write the -ing form of six verbs applying the spelling rules, and (2) write 4 sentences about what the people in their home or street are doing right now, using am/is/are + -ing and a time marker (now / at the moment / today).
