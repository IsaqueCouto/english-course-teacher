Lesson 16 — Filling In Forms (IELTS Listening Section 1)

How to teach this

This lesson is a gentle drip of IELTS Listening — specifically Section 1 — combined with a very practical real-life skill: filling in forms.

What IELTS Listening Section 1 is: the first part of the Listening test is almost always a conversation between two people in an everyday situation — for example, someone booking a room, registering at a clinic, opening an account, or signing up for a class. As they talk, the listener completes a form or a set of notes: writing down names (often spelled out letter by letter), numbers (phone numbers, prices), dates, and addresses. This is the easiest part of the test and the closest to real life, which makes it a kind first taste.

The whole lesson is listen → write and read → write. You read the conversation aloud; the student completes the form on their sheet. The student does not speak in this lesson — they only listen, write, and read.

How to read the dialogue:

  • Tell the student what to listen for before you read (names, a phone number, a date, an address). The form on their sheet already shows them what is coming.
  • Read the conversation slowly and clearly, TWICE. First read: the student just listens and follows the form with their eyes. Second read: the student writes in the blanks.
  • In the real IELTS test the audio plays only once. We read twice here on purpose so the student feels supported. You can mention this gently.
  • Read both speakers (the clerk and the customer). A small change of voice helps, but keep it natural and unhurried.
  • For a spelled-out name, say each letter with a small pause: "M — O — R — A — L — E — S". For a phone number, group the digits and pause between groups.
  • For dates, say them naturally ("the fourth of March, nineteen ninety"). Accept any clear written form.
  • Light Portuguese gloss is fine for new ideas: formulário = form; sobrenome = surname; soletrar = to spell.

The big idea to plant (PT): Numa conversa, você ouve e escreve as informações pessoais — nome, sobrenome soletrado, telefone, data, endereço. É exatamente isso que a Seção 1 do IELTS Listening pede.

Objective

By the end of the lesson the student can complete a form by catching personal details from a spoken conversation — a name, a surname spelled out letter by letter, a phone number, a date of birth, and an address — which is the core IELTS Listening Section 1 skill. The student can also read a simple paper form and answer questions about it in writing. For more on the test, you may point the student to ielts/ielts-overview.md; for ear training, see ielts/accent-listening.md.

Read-aloud dialogue

Read this slowly, twice. The bold items are the key facts the student writes on the form. Do not read the bold formatting or the bracketed glosses aloud — just speak the words naturally. The student sheet has matching blanks, with the first field (First name) already filled in as an example.

A (clerk): "Good morning. I'd like to register you as a new member. Can I take some details? What's your first name?" B (customer): "Yes, of course. My first name is Lucas." A: "Thank you. And your surname?" B: "It's Morales. Shall I spell that for you?" A: "Yes, please." B: "M — O — R — A — L — E — S, Morales." A: "Lovely. And a phone number where we can reach you?" B: "It's 0-7-7-0-0, then nine-double-two, four-one-six." A: "Let me read that back — 07700, 922 416. And your date of birth?" B: "The fourth of March, nineteen ninety — so the fourth of the third, 1990." A: "Great. What's your address?" B: "It's number fifty-two, Maple Road. The town is Hartwell." A: "Thank you. And finally, what's the reason for your visit today?" B: "I'd like to join the swimming classes, please."

Listen for: first name (Lucas), surname spelled (MORALES), phone (07700 922 416), date of birth (4 March 1990 / 04/03/1990), house number (52), street (Maple Road), town (Hartwell), reason for visit (swimming classes).

Optional gentle note: "double-two" means two twos (2-2). Gloss in PT if needed: "double" = dois iguais seguidos. If the student misses it, re-read that one group once.

Flow with timings (~45–50 min)

Warm-up (5 min) — In Portuguese, set the scene: imagine you arrive somewhere new — a clinic, a gym, a class — and a clerk asks for your details and writes them on a form. Today we practise catching exactly those details. Remind: no speaking — just listening, writing, and reading.

Present (8 min) — Show the student sheet. Walk through the worked EXAMPLE (First name = Lucas) so the student sees how a caught detail goes in the blank. Point out the kinds of details to expect: a surname will be spelled, there will be a phone number, a date, an address. Explain you will read the conversation twice — first just listen, second time write.

Practice — listen & write the form (18 min) — Read the full dialogue twice, slowly, reading both speakers. Tell the student before the first read which details are coming. After the second read, give time to finish writing. Then read the short note-completion (on the sheet) twice as well. Do not show answers yet.

Produce — read & write (12 min) — The student does the reading task on their sheet alone (silently): they read a simple completed application form and answer three questions in writing. This shifts from catching spoken details to reading written ones.

Wrap-up (5–7 min) — Go through all answers together, gently. Praise correct catches, especially the spelled surname and the phone number. For anything missed, re-read that single line once and let the student fix it. Check the reading answers. End with encouragement: completing a form from a conversation is exactly what IELTS Listening Section 1 asks, and the student is already doing it.

Common mistakes & gentle corrections

  • Spelled surname missed or mis-ordered. A Portuguese speaker may expect Portuguese letter names and can confuse, for example, English "E" with Portuguese "I". If a letter is missed, re-read that single letter clearly once. Accept the correctly written surname even if it was caught letter by letter unevenly.
  • Phone-number traps: "double". "Double two" = 2-2. If the student writes a single digit, re-read the group once. Accept the digits written together (07700 922 416) in any sensible grouping.
  • "Thirteen" vs "thirty" / "fifty-two" vs "twenty-five". These endings confuse beginners. If missed, re-read slowly, stressing the ending. Accept the digits (52) or the words.
  • Date format. "The fourth of March 1990" = 4 March 1990, 04/03/1990, or 4th March 1990 — all fine. Note gently that in much of the English-speaking world the day comes before the month (04/03 = 4 March), which differs from US-style month-first. Do not require one fixed format today.
  • Address. Accept "52 Maple Road" or the parts written separately. The town (Hartwell) is a separate field — watch that the student does not merge it into the street.
  • Reason for visit. Accept any clear short answer that means joining the swimming classes ("swimming", "swimming classes", "to swim").
  • Always correct gently and in writing — point, re-read once, let the student fix it. No pressure, no comment on speed.

Homework

Set the at-home listening from ielts/accent-listening.md — short, gentle listening to get the ear used to natural English. Remind the student: just listen for the key facts (names, numbers, dates, addresses), not every word.

On the student sheet there is also a small written homework task: a blank form for the student to fill in on their own (silently) with their own real details, so the form-filling habit continues between lessons. Bring the completed sheet next time.
