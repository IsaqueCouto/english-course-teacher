Lesson 12 — Shopping, Money, and Prices

How to teach this

This is a practical survival lesson. The student is moving abroad for work and will need to shop, read prices and receipts, and ask simple questions in stores. Keep everything reading, listening (you read aloud), writing, and recognition. The student does not speak in this lesson — all production happens on paper. When you want to model spoken language, you read it and the student listens or follows along on the sheet, then writes.

Go slowly and praise often. The student already reads and writes simple sentences and knows a/an, plurals, and the polite "I'd like". This lesson recycles all of that inside a shopping context. Every exercise on the student sheet opens with a solved example — point to it first, confirm the student understands the pattern, then let them write. Use the light Portuguese glosses only when the student looks stuck; do not translate everything.

The one grammar focus that needs care is "How much is the…?" (one thing) vs. "How much are the…?" (more than one thing). Treat this as the heart of the lesson and return to it in the error-correction stage.

Objective

By the end, the student can:

  • understand common shopping vocabulary;
  • read prices, sizes, and a simple receipt or price list;
  • write simple shopping questions and sentences: "How much is…?", "How much are…?", "I'd like…", "Do you have…?", "Can I pay by card?".

Target language

Vocabulary (~12):

  • shop / store
  • price
  • cheap / expensive
  • size (small / medium / large)
  • cash
  • card
  • change (the money you get back)
  • receipt
  • discount / sale
  • "How much is…?" / "How much are…?"
  • "I'd like…"
  • "Can I pay by card?"

Recycled (already known): a / an, regular plurals, the polite "I'd like".

Grammar focus: is with one item, are with two or more items, after "How much".

Note on Portuguese for the teacher: in Portuguese the student likely thinks "Quanto custa…?" / "Quanto custam…?" — note that Portuguese also changes the verb for singular vs. plural (custa / custam), so the English is/are split maps cleanly onto something familiar. "I'd like" = "Eu queria / Gostaria de"; "change" (troco) is a different word from the verb "to change". Use "$" as a neutral currency symbol throughout; do not name a country.

Flow with timings (~45–50 min)

Warm-up (~5 min)

Show the student the Key language list on the sheet. Read each word aloud while the student reads along silently and looks at the Portuguese gloss. Ask the student to point to (recognition only) the words for "cheap", "expensive", and "receipt". No speaking.

Present (~10 min)

On paper or a whiteboard, write three small things and two of the same thing, e.g.:

  • one apple → How much is the apple?
  • three apples → How much are the apples? Read both aloud. Draw a clear box around is and are. Then write "I'd like a medium coffee." and "Can I pay by card?" and read them. Confirm the student recognises a/an and the plural -s. Keep it visual.

Practice (~18 min)

Work through exercises (a)–(d) on the student sheet. For each: read the solved example aloud, check understanding, then the student writes the answers. Circulate and help with the glosses if needed.

  • (a) prices: match the price to the item / write a price in words.
  • (b) is/are: complete the shopping questions.
  • (c) receipt reading: the student reads the short receipt and answers four questions in writing.
  • (d) error correction: 3–4 clear mistakes to fix in writing.

Produce — student WRITES (~10 min)

Exercise (e): the student writes a short 4–5 line shopping dialogue, completing the customer's lines using the sentence starters. Remind the student this is a writing task only. Read the finished dialogue aloud for them at the end so they hear it, but they do not speak it.

Wrap-up (~3 min)

Read the Key language list aloud one more time while the student follows. Point to the is/are box. Hand out / confirm the homework.

Common mistakes & gentle corrections

  • "How much is the shoes?" → shoes is plural, so "How much are the shoes?" Gently underline the -s and the verb together.
  • "How much are the milk?" → milk is one thing here, so "How much is the milk?"
  • Using "I like" instead of "I'd like" when ordering → remind that the polite form for shopping/ordering is "I'd like…". ("I like coffee" = a general preference; "I'd like a coffee" = a request now.)
  • Confusing "change" (the money back, o troco) with "cheap" → keep them apart with a quick reread of the glosses.
  • Writing the price symbol after the number ("5$") → in this course we write "$5" with the symbol first.

Always re-read the corrected sentence aloud for the student so they hear the right version; they do not repeat it.

Homework

On the student sheet. The student writes:

  1. four shopping questions using "How much is…?" and "How much are…?" (two of each);
  2. a short three-line note about a small shopping trip ("I'd like…", which item is cheap or expensive, did they pay by cash or card). Sentence starters are provided. Reading and writing only.
