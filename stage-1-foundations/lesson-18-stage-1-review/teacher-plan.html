Lesson 18 — Stage 1 Review and Progress Check

How to teach this

This is the last lesson of Stage 1. It is a mixed review and a gentle progress check. Like every lesson, it is a reading and writing lesson — the student does not speak at any point. Every task is reading, listening while you read aloud, recognition, or writing. Do not ask the student to say, repeat, or read anything aloud.

Treat this lesson as a friendly check, not a hard test. The goal is to see what the student already controls in writing and to notice the one or two areas that still need a little more practice before Stage 2. Each exercise on the student sheet opens with a solved example, so the student always sees the pattern before they write. Keep light Portuguese glosses ready to unlock meaning, but let the student write in English as much as possible. Reward correct written sentences and correct gently.

Have paper and a pen ready. The student writes everything by hand. You read short texts aloud while the student follows on the page.

Objective

By the end, the student can demonstrate, in writing, control of the core Stage 1 grammar — the present simple (including he/she/it with -s), questions, there is / there are, the present continuous, the past simple (regular and irregular), and the future (going to and will) — and can produce a short mixed-tense paragraph about themselves using present, past, and future together.

What this reviews

This lesson pulls together everything from Stage 1.

Grammar:

  • The present simple for routines and facts (I work, they live).
  • He / she / it and the -s ending (she works, he lives).
  • Questions (Do you...? Does she...? Where...? What...?).
  • There is / there are for saying what exists.
  • The present continuous for actions happening now (I am working).
  • The past simple, regular verbs (worked, lived, studied).
  • The past simple, irregular verbs (went, had, saw, came).
  • The future with going to (plans) and will (decisions now, predictions, offers).

Practical and IELTS-style topics from the stage:

  • Listening for key words while you read aloud.
  • Housing and rooms.
  • Reading signs and notices.
  • Getting around (transport, directions).
  • Shopping and prices.
  • Describing people and places.
  • Workplace small talk.
  • Filling in forms.
  • Messages and notes.

Flow with timings (~45–50 min)

Warm-up (5 min). Write four short sentences on paper, one for each main tense: "I work in a shop." / "She is working now." / "We went to the market yesterday." / "I am going to call you tomorrow." Ask the student to read them silently and, in writing, label each one: PRESENT, NOW, PAST, or FUTURE. Confirm answers together. This wakes up the tense system before the review begins.

Review carousel (15 min). The student works through the Mixed Review on the student sheet — short sections that pull two or three items from each Stage 1 grammar point. Each section begins with a solved example. Move through them in order; do not let the student get stuck. If a section is clearly hard, make a quiet mental note (this is the progress-check part) and move on. Circulate and check as the student writes.

Practice (10 min). The student reads the short text on the student sheet (you may also read it aloud once while the student follows) and answers the comprehension questions in writing, in full sentences. This blends the practical topics (describing a person, routine, a past event, a plan) with the grammar.

Produce — student WRITES (12 min). The student completes Write about yourself — a paragraph of about six sentences using present, past, and future, built on the sentence frame. This is the main output and the clearest signal of readiness for Stage 2. Encourage true, real sentences about the student's own life. Let them take their time.

Wrap-up (5 min). Read the student's paragraph aloud yourself while the student follows on the page. Give gentle written corrections. Recap, in one written line, the three tenses they just used: present = agora / rotina; past = ontem / antes; future = depois / plano. Set the homework.

Ready for Stage 2?

Use the student's written work today to judge readiness. Stage 2 builds on solid Stage 1 foundations, so look for control, not perfection.

The student is ready to move on if, in writing, they can:

  • Use the present simple correctly, including the -s on he / she / it most of the time.
  • Form a simple question with do / does or a question word.
  • Use there is / there are correctly with singular and plural.
  • Tell the difference, in writing, between a routine (present simple) and an action now (present continuous).
  • Put regular and irregular verbs into the past simple, getting the common irregulars (went, had, saw) right more often than not.
  • Choose going to or will for a clear plan or prediction.
  • Write a short paragraph that moves between present, past, and future without losing the meaning.

Small, occasional slips (a missing -s, one wrong irregular verb) are normal and fine — the student is ready. If a whole area is still shaky (for example, the past simple is mostly guesswork, or questions collapse), spend one extra short session on just that topic before starting Stage 2, rather than holding the whole stage back.

When you have decided, update 00-start-here/progress-tracker.md: mark Stage 1 as complete, note today's date, and write a one-line note on any topic to keep an eye on in Stage 2.

Common mistakes & gentle corrections

  • "She work in a shop." → "She works in a shop." (Add -s for he / she / it.) PT: terceira pessoa leva -s.
  • "Do she works here?" → "Does she work here?" (Use does + base verb; the -s moves to does.) PT: em pergunta com does, o verbo fica na forma base.
  • "There is two rooms." → "There are two rooms." (Plural needs are.) PT: plural pede there are.
  • "I working now." → "I am working now." (Present continuous needs be + -ing.) PT: presente contínuo precisa de am/is/are.
  • "Yesterday I go to the market." → "Yesterday I went to the market." (Past simple, irregular.) PT: ontem = passado; go vira went.
  • "She goed home." → "She went home." (Irregular, not -ed.) PT: go é irregular, não leva -ed.
  • "I will to call you." → "I will call you." (No to after will.) PT: depois de will, verbo base sem to.
  • "She going to work tomorrow." → "She is going to work tomorrow." (Going to needs be.) PT: going to precisa de am/is/are.

Homework

On the student sheet: (1) the student rewrites four mixed sentences into the form asked (negative, question, or a different tense), and (2) the student writes a second short paragraph (about six sentences) about a family member or a friend — who they are, their routine now, something they did last week, and a plan for the future — reusing the same mix of present, past, and future. Bring the written work to the first Stage 2 lesson.
