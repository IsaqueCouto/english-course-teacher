Lesson 10 — Past Simple: Irregular Verbs

How to teach this

This lesson follows the regular past simple. The student already knows the regular past with -ed and how to make negatives with didn't. Build directly on that: irregular verbs are the same idea (one past form for all subjects), but the past form is not made with -ed — it has to be learned as a word.

Keep the whole lesson on the page. The student does NOT speak at any point. Every task is reading, recognition, or writing. When the plan says "listening", that means YOU read a sentence aloud and the student WRITES what they hear — never the reverse.

Two big ideas to land:

  1. Irregular pasts are memorized forms (go → went, not "goed").
  2. The "didn't" trap: after didn't (and after did in questions), the verb goes back to its BASE form. So the irregular past disappears there. "I went" → "I didn't go". "Did you go?" not "Did you went?"

Objective

The student can write common irregular past-simple verbs and use them to describe past events. Negatives still use didn't + base verb, so the irregular past form is NOT used after didn't.

Target language

Irregular verbs (base → past):

Base Past
be was / were
go went
have had
do did
get got
make made
see saw
come came
take took
eat ate
give gave
say said
find found
buy bought

Sentence patterns:

  • Positive: I went to work yesterday.
  • Negative: I didn't go to work yesterday. (base verb returns)
  • Question: Did you go to work? (base verb returns)

PT note for the teacher: in Portuguese the past is also irregular for many verbs (ir → fui, ter → tive, fazer → fiz), so the concept of irregular pasts is familiar. The difference to flag: in English the past form is the same for every subject (I/you/he/she/we/they → went), unlike the changing endings in Portuguese.

Flow with timings (~45–50 min)

Warm-up (5 min) Write three regular past sentences on the page with one verb each in base form (e.g. work, play, watch). Student fills the -ed past in writing. This recalls what they already know before the new irregular forms appear.

Present (10 min) Walk through the irregular table on the student sheet. For each pair, point out: no -ed, just a new word. Group a few helpful pairs visually (go/went, see/saw, eat/ate, take/took). Then write "I goed home" on the page, cross it out, and write "I went home" beside it so the contrast is concrete. Finish by writing the negative pair: I went → I didn't go. Underline the base verb returning.

Practice (15 min) Student works through exercises (a), (b), (c) on the sheet — each opens with a solved example. Let them use the table as a reference; this is recognition and supported writing, not memory testing. Check answers in writing together.

Produce — student WRITES (12 min) Student does exercises (d) the "didn't" trap, (e) error correction, (f) read-and-answer, and starts (g) writing about a memorable day using the sentence starters. The student writes everything; you read the short story in (f) aloud once if helpful while they follow the text. Do not ask the student to speak.

Wrap-up (5 min) Quick written check: dictate (you read, student writes) two short sentences using irregular pasts, e.g. "She had lunch at home." and "We saw a film." Confirm spelling against the table.

Common mistakes & gentle corrections

  • Regularizing the verb: "She goed home." → Gently: go is irregular — the past is went. "She went home." Point back to the table.
  • Double past in negatives: "He didn't went." → After didn't the verb goes back to base: didn't go. "He didn't go."
  • Double past in questions: "Did you went?" → Same rule with did: Did you go? Underline the base verb.
  • was/were mix-up: "They was here." → Plural subjects take were: "They were here." (Keep this light — one example is enough.)
  • Spelling slips: bought, said, found are commonly misspelled. Praise the correct grammar first, then fix spelling against the table.

Correct warmly and always show the right form in writing right next to the error.

Homework

Set the homework section on the student sheet: complete any unfinished sentences in (g), and write 4 short sentences using four different irregular verbs from the table (two positive, two negative with didn't). Remind the student in writing that the negative ones use the base verb.
