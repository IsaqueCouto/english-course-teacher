Lesson 14 — Describing People and Places

How to teach this

This lesson is reading, listening, and writing only. The student does not speak. You read examples and prompts aloud; the student reads silently, recognizes, matches, and writes. Keep the pace gentle. Every exercise starts with one solved example, so point to that example first and make sure the student understands the pattern before they try the rest. Word banks and sentence starters are there to remove pressure — encourage the student to lean on them.

The single most important idea is adjective position. In Portuguese the adjective usually comes after the noun ("uma casa grande"), but in English it comes before ("a big house"). Return to this point often and gently.

Objective

The student can use common adjectives to describe people (appearance and personality) and places, with correct adjective position: before the noun ("a big house") and after the verb "be" ("The house is big").

Target language

Adjectives (about 12-14):

  • Appearance: tall, short, young, old, thin
  • Personality: kind, friendly, funny, serious, quiet
  • Places: big, small, clean, busy, beautiful, modern (plus "old" and "quiet" reused from above)

Rule 1 — Position:

  • Before a noun: a tall man / a clean room
  • After "be": He is tall. / The room is clean.

Rule 2 — Order (kept light): when two or three adjectives go together, the usual order is opinion + size + age, for example "a nice small old house". Teach this lightly as a pattern to recognize, not a rule to master.

Grammar trap (usage): English puts the adjective BEFORE the noun ("a big house"); Portuguese often puts it after ("uma casa grande"). This is the most common mistake — flag it warmly every time.

Flow with timings (~45–50 min)

Warm-up (5 min). Read out a few simple descriptions and have the student point to or write the matching word. For example, you read "not tall" and the student writes "short"; you read "not big" and the student writes "small". This activates opposites with no speaking.

Present (10 min). Walk through the Key language on the student sheet. Group the adjectives (appearance / personality / places). Then teach the two positions side by side on paper:

  • a kind person (before the noun)
  • The person is kind. (after "be")

Write the Portuguese contrast clearly: "uma casa grande" vs "a big house". Underline that English flips the order.

Practice (15 min). Student works through exercises (a)–(d) on the sheet: putting the adjective in the right place, matching opposites, choosing the right adjective from a word bank, and correcting clear errors. Each has a solved example. Check answers together by reading them; the student confirms by pointing or writing.

Produce — student WRITES (10–12 min). Exercise (e): the student reads a short description and writes answers to the questions. Then exercise (f): using the sentence starters, the student writes 4–5 sentences describing a person they know and a place they like. This is the main writing output. Circulate and help with spelling and adjective position.

Wrap-up (5 min). Read a few of the student's sentences silently together. Point to any adjective that is in the wrong place and let the student fix it in writing. End by re-reading the rule "the adjective comes before the noun".

Common mistakes & gentle corrections

  • Adjective after the noun (transfer from Portuguese): "a house big" → guide to "a big house". This is the key trap; expect it and correct it gently every time.
  • Adjective after the noun with "a person": "She is a person kind" → "She is a kind person".
  • Adding -s to the adjective: "two bigs houses" → "two big houses". Adjectives never take a plural -s in English.
  • Wrong word for the meaning: choosing "busy" for an empty street, etc. Re-read the description and let the student choose again.

Correct by showing the right form in writing next to the student's version, not by asking them to speak. A quick "almost — in English we say it like this" plus the corrected sentence works well.

Homework

On the student sheet: the student writes a short description (4–5 sentences) of a family member or friend, using at least four adjectives from the lesson, and a 2–3 sentence description of a place near where they live. They underline every adjective and check that each one is in the right place (before the noun, or after "be").
