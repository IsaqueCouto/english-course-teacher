Lesson 9 — Past Simple: Regular Verbs

How to teach this

This lesson is about WRITING the past simple of regular verbs and spelling them correctly. The student does not speak at any point. All "listening" steps mean YOU read a sentence aloud and the student WRITES what they hear. All other steps are reading, recognition, and writing.

Keep the focus on the four spelling patterns and on the negative with didn't. The student already writes simple present-tense sentences well, so build gently from there: show the present verb, then the past form, then let the student produce it in writing. Use short Portuguese glosses only to explain a rule, never to translate whole tasks.

The biggest trap in this lesson is usage, not spelling: after didn't, the verb returns to the base form (no -ed). Flag this clearly and return to it in the wrap-up.

Objective

By the end, the student can:

  • write the past simple of regular verbs with correct spelling (the four -ed patterns);
  • write sentences about yesterday / last week using past time markers;
  • form negatives with didn't + base verb.

Target language

The four -ed spelling rules

  • Most verbs: add -ed → work → worked, watch → watched, visit → visited, call → called, walk → walked, finish → finished, start → started, play → played, want → wanted, clean → cleaned, cook → cooked.
  • Verbs already ending in -e: add just -d → like → liked.
  • Verbs ending in consonant + y: change y → ied → study → studied.
  • Short verbs ending in one vowel + one consonant: double the consonant + ed → stop → stopped.

Past time markers: yesterday, last night, last week, two days ago, in 2019.

Negative: didn't + base verb → "I didn't work." (NOT "I didn't worked.")

Verbs used (~12): work, study, watch, cook, clean, visit, call, walk, finish, start, play, want.

Flow with timings (~45–50 min)

Warm-up (5 min) — Write three present-tense sentences the student already knows (e.g. "I work every day."). Ask the student to underline the verb in each. This refreshes the idea of a verb before we change its time.

Present (10 min) — On paper, present the four spelling rules one at a time. For each rule, write the present form and the past form side by side (work → worked; like → liked; study → studied; stop → stopped). Give the short PT gloss: o passado regular termina em -ed; muda só a escrita. Then introduce the time markers in a list and explain that past tense goes with these words.

Practice (15 min) — Student works through Exercises (a)–(d) on the student sheet in writing. Each one starts with a solved example. Circulate and point at the relevant rule when a spelling slips. For Exercise (d) error correction, let the student find the errors before you confirm.

Produce — student WRITES (10 min) — Listening-to-writing: read 3 simple past sentences slowly, twice each, and the student WRITES them (e.g. "She cooked dinner last night."). Then the student does Exercise (f): 4–5 sentences about what they did yesterday or last weekend, using the sentence starters.

Wrap-up (5 min) — Return to the didn't trap. Write "She didn't worked." and ask the student to correct it in writing → "She didn't work." Quick recap of the four spelling rules.

Common mistakes & gentle corrections

  • "studyed" / "studyied" → point to the consonant + y → ied rule: study → studied. Gently: "Cubra o -y e escreva -ied."
  • "stoped" → short verb, double the consonant: stop → stopped.
  • "likeed" → the verb already ends in -e, so add just -d: liked.
  • "didn't worked" → after didn't, the verb is the base form: didn't work. This is the key usage point — correct it warmly every time it appears.
  • Mixing present and past ("Yesterday I work") → the time marker yesterday needs the past form worked.

Homework

On the student sheet: complete the spelling table, write 4 past sentences from prompts, and correct 3 sentences that use the wrong form after didn't. The student writes everything; nothing is spoken.
