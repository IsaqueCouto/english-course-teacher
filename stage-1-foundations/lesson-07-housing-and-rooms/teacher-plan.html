Lesson 7 — Housing: Renting a Place

How to teach this

This lesson gives the student the words and sentences they need to find and rent a place to live abroad. It is a practical survival topic, so keep every example concrete and useful.

Remember: the student never speaks. Every task is reading, listening (you read, the student writes), recognition, or writing. Never ask the student to say, repeat, pronounce, or read aloud.

When you do the listening parts, read the sentence or word slowly, twice, and the student writes what they hear. Give a light Portuguese gloss for each new word the first time it appears, then encourage the student to rely on English. Praise correct writing; correct gently and in writing.

Objective

By the end of the lesson, the student can:

  • understand common housing and renting vocabulary,
  • read a simple rental advertisement and answer questions about it in writing,
  • write basic sentences and questions about a place to live.

Target language

Vocabulary (~12 items), each with a short PT gloss for the student sheet:

  • apartment / flat — apartamento (both words mean the same; "flat" is common in some countries)
  • house — casa
  • room — quarto / cômodo
  • rent (noun) — o aluguel; rent (verb) — alugar
  • landlord — o senhorio / proprietário (the person who owns the place and rents it to you)
  • tenant — o inquilino (the person who pays rent and lives there)
  • deposit — o depósito / caução (money you pay first, and get back later if nothing is broken)
  • furnished / unfurnished — mobiliado / sem mobília
  • utilities (water, electricity) — serviços (água, eletricidade)
  • bills — as contas
  • lease / contract — o contrato (de aluguel)
  • neighbour — o vizinho / a vizinha

Recycle from earlier work: there is / there are ("There is a kitchen. There are two rooms.") and prepositions for describing a place (in, near, next to, with).

Flow with timings (~45–50 min)

Warm-up (5 min) — recognition Write four words on paper: house, room, rent, neighbour. Next to them, write short Portuguese glosses in a mixed order. The student draws a line matching each English word to its meaning. This activates the topic with no speaking.

Present (10 min) — light input Go through the Key language box on the student sheet. For each word, point, give the short PT gloss once, and write one tiny example sentence ("The rent is high." / "The apartment is furnished."). Make the difference between landlord and tenant, and between furnished and unfurnished, very clear with a quick drawing or contrast. Confirm understanding by having the student write the matching English word next to two or three pictures or glosses you point to.

Practice (12 min) — controlled writing Do Exercise (a) together first (matching / fill-in with a word bank — there is a solved example). Then the student reads the rental ad in Exercise (b) and writes answers to the 4 questions. Read the ad aloud once slowly for support before they answer (this is listening support; the student still only writes).

Produce (12–15 min) — the student WRITES Exercise (c): the student writes questions to ask a landlord, using the sentence starters. Exercise (d): error correction — 3–4 clear errors to fix in writing. Exercise (e): the student writes 4–5 sentences describing the kind of place they want to rent, using the sentence starters. Circulate and correct gently in writing.

Wrap-up (5 min) — recognition check Point to 5 vocabulary words at random; the student writes the PT gloss (or the matching English word). Confirm the homework.

Common mistakes & gentle corrections

  • Confusing landlord and tenant. Gentle fix: write "owner = landlord, renter = tenant" and let the student re-match.
  • Writing "I want rent an apartment." → guide to "I want to rent an apartment." (verb needs to).
  • Saying "How much the rent is?" in writing → model the question word order: "How much is the rent?"
  • Using "there is" with plurals: "There is two rooms." → "There are two rooms."
  • Treating "flat" and "apartment" as different things — reassure they are the same.
  • Forgetting the deposit is returnable money, not the rent. A one-line PT gloss usually fixes this.

Homework

On the student sheet: the student (1) labels a simple drawing of a place using 6 vocabulary words, and (2) writes 4 sentences about the place where they live now (or would like to live), using there is / there are and the sentence starters. Writing only.
