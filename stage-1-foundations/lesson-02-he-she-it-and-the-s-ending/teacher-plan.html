Lesson 2 — He / She / It and the -s Ending

How to teach this

This lesson builds directly on Lesson 1 (present simple for I / you / we / they). The new idea is small but important: when the subject is he, she, or it, the verb usually takes an -s. Recycle the routine verbs from Lesson 1 (get up, work, study, eat, watch, go, have, etc.) so the student is learning one new rule, not new vocabulary.

The student WRITES throughout — never speaks. All tasks are reading, recognition, sorting, transforming, and writing. You do all the talking and modelling. Speaking is coached separately, not here.

Keep it gentle and scaffolded:

  • Every exercise on the student sheet starts with a worked example that is already done.
  • The spelling rules and word banks are printed for the student to look at while writing.
  • The final "write your own" task gives sentence starters and a frame.

Give the short Portuguese gloss when you introduce each rule, then switch back to English. Errors you correct are obvious ones (a missing -s, or works after doesn't).

Objective

By the end, the student can write present-simple sentences about a third person (he / she / it) with the correct -s / -es / -ies spelling, and can write negatives with doesn't + base verb.

Target language

  • Recycled Lesson 1 verbs in the third person singular: gets up, works, studies, eats, watches, goes, has, does, finishes, lives, reads, drinks.
  • Spelling rules:
    • Most verbs: add -s → work → works, eat → eats, live → lives.
    • Verbs ending in -ch, -sh, -ss, -x, -o: add -es → watch → watches, finish → finishes, go → goes.
    • Consonant + y: change y → ies → study → studies. (But play → plays, because there is a vowel before the y.)
  • Irregulars to memorise: have → has, go → goes, do → does.
  • Negative: doesn't + base verb → She doesn't work on Sunday.
  • Frequency adverbs still go before the main verb: He always works late.
  • Contrast: don't = I / you / we / they; doesn't = he / she / it.

Flow with timings (~45–50 min)

Warm-up (5 min)

Write three Lesson-12 sentences on the board: I work. You study. We watch TV. Ask the student to write the same three sentences but starting with She. Don't explain yet — let them try, then use what they wrote to introduce the -s. (Expect some missing -s; that is the hook.)

Present (12 min)

Present the rule clearly. On the board, build two columns: I / you / we / they → work and he / she / it → works. State the gloss: "Com he, she, it, o verbo quase sempre ganha -s." Then show the three spelling patterns with examples (watch→watches, study→studies). Show the three irregulars (has, goes, does). Finally show the negative: cross out the -s and replace it — She doesn't work (base form, no -s after doesn't). Gloss: "Depois de doesn't, o verbo volta à forma base, sem -s."

Practice (18 min)

The student works through the student-sheet exercises in order: write the he/she form of verbs (a), sort the endings (b), transform I → He/She (c), correct the obvious errors (d), make sentences negative with doesn't (e), and read-and-answer (f). Each one starts from its solved example. Circulate, point at the printed rules when they hesitate, and praise correct -s endings.

Produce — student writes ~4–5 sentences (15 min)

The student writes a short paragraph about a family member or friend (third person) using the frame and sentence starters on the sheet (task g). They WRITE; they do not speak. Encourage them to use at least one negative with doesn't and one frequency adverb.

Wrap-up (5 min)

Dictation — short full sentences. You say each sentence once slowly; the student writes it. Then check the -s endings together on paper.

  1. She works in a hospital.
  2. He studies English every day.
  3. My brother goes to the gym on Saturday.
  4. She doesn't watch TV in the morning.

Common mistakes & gentle corrections

  • Missing -s on he/she: "She work in a shop" → gently underline the verb and ask what we add for she. Correct: She works in a shop.
  • -s kept after doesn't: "He doesn't works" → remind: after doesn't, the verb goes back to base form. Correct: He doesn't work.
  • Wrong spelling rule: "She studys" → point to the consonant + y rule. Correct: She studies. And "He watchs" → ends in -ch, so add -es: He watches.
  • don't / doesn't mix-up: "She don't eat meat" → don't is for I/you/we/they; for she use doesn't. Correct: She doesn't eat meat.

Keep corrections light: show the rule, let the student rewrite the sentence themselves.

Homework

On the student sheet (Homework section): the student rewrites a short set of I sentences into He/She form, fixes two more obvious errors, and writes 3 sentences about a second person (a neighbour, a coworker, or a friend) using he or she, including one doesn't sentence. Bring it to the next lesson for written feedback.
