Lesson 5 — Listening for Key Words

How to teach this

This is the student's first gentle taste of IELTS Listening. Keep the mood light and low-pressure — the goal is a positive first experience, not a test.

The whole lesson is listen → write. You read each short script aloud; the student writes what they catch. The student does not speak in this lesson — they only listen and write.

Key teaching method for every script:

  • Tell the student what kind of information to listen for before you read (e.g. "Listen for a name and a number").
  • Read each script slowly and clearly, TWICE. First read: the student just listens. Second read: the student writes.
  • In the real IELTS test the audio plays only once, but for this first lesson you read twice on purpose, so the student feels supported. You can mention this gently so the student understands you are making it easier today.
  • Do not rush. Pause naturally at commas and full stops.
  • If the student misses something, smile, re-read that one line once more, and move on. No pressure, no correction of "speed".
  • Light Portuguese gloss is fine for new ideas (palavra-chave = key word).

The big idea to plant: you do not need to catch every word — just the key facts. In Portuguese: Não precisa entender cada palavra. Só precisa pegar as informações importantes — nomes, números, horas, lugares.

Objective

By the end of the lesson the student can catch and write key information — names (including spelled-out names), numbers, times, dates, and places/addresses — from short spoken texts. This is the core IELTS Listening skill.

What IELTS Listening is (brief)

IELTS Listening is one of the four parts of the test. The student hears recordings (conversations and short talks) and answers questions while listening. A very common task type is filling in blanks in notes or a form — exactly what we practise today: catching a name, a number, a time, a place.

In the real test the recording plays once only, and the accents vary. For more detail, point the student to ielts/ielts-overview.md (what the four parts are and how Listening is scored) and ielts/accent-listening.md (getting used to different English accents).

Today we make it gentle: you read twice, the texts are short, and the student is told in advance what to listen for.

Read-aloud scripts

Read each script slowly, twice. The bold items are the key facts the student must catch and write. Do not read the bold formatting aloud — just say the words naturally. (The student sheet has matching blanks.)

Tip before you start: for spelled-out names, say each letter clearly with a small pause: "M — A — R — I — A". For phone numbers, group the digits and pause between groups.

Script A — a name and a number (shortest; warm-up)

"Hello. My name is Carla. My phone number is two-six-eight, four-one-five-five."

Listen for: the name, the phone number (268 4155).

Script B — a time and a place (2 sentences)

"The English class is on Monday. It starts at half past six in the evening. The room is number twelve."

Listen for: the day (Monday), the time (6:30 / half past six), the room number (12).

Script C — a spelled-out name and an address (2–3 sentences)

"Good morning. My name is Diego — that is spelled D — I — E — G — O. I live at number forty-seven, Green Street. I am from Mexico."

Listen for: the spelled name (DIEGO), the house number (47), the street (Green Street), the country (Mexico).

Script D — numbers, a date, and a price (3 sentences; longest)

"Welcome to the library. It opens at nine o'clock in the morning and closes at eight in the evening. The library card is free for students, but a lost card costs five dollars. Your card is ready on the third of June."

Listen for: open time (9:00), close time (8:00), the price (5 dollars / $5), the date (3 June / 3rd of June).

Note-completion dialogue — a short booking (4–5 lines; read both speakers)

Read this as a small conversation. Read both parts. The student fills a note grid on their sheet.

A: "Hello, I'd like to book a room, please." B: "Of course. What is your name?" A: "It's Sofia Ramos — Ramos is R — A — M — O — S." B: "Thank you. And what day would you like?" A: "Friday, at ten o'clock in the morning. For three people, please."

Listen for: name (Sofia Ramos), spelling of surname (RAMOS), day (Friday), time (10:00), number of people (3).

Flow with timings (~45–50 min)

Warm-up (5 min) — Lightly introduce the idea in Portuguese: today we listen and catch the important facts, not every word. No speaking needed — just listening and writing. Mention this is a first, easy look at IELTS Listening.

Present (8 min) — Show the student sheet. Walk through the worked EXAMPLE row together so they see exactly what "catch the key word and write it" means. Explain: you will read each script twice; first time just listen, second time write.

Practice (20 min) — Read Scripts A → B → C → D in order, each one twice, slowly. After each script, give a few seconds for writing, then move on. Tell the student before each one what to listen for. Do not show answers yet.

Produce — listen & write (10 min) — Read the note-completion dialogue (both speakers), twice. The student completes the note grid. This is the "test-like" moment, but still supported (read twice).

Wrap-up (5–7 min) — Go back through the answers together gently. Praise what was caught correctly. For anything missed, re-read that single line once and let the student fill it in. End on encouragement: this is exactly the skill IELTS Listening tests, and the student is already doing it.

Common mistakes & gentle corrections

  • Trying to write every word. Remind them (PT): só as palavras-chave. If they freeze trying to catch a whole sentence, point at the blank and name what to listen for ("just the number here").
  • Numbers: "half past six" vs "six thirty". Both are correct. Accept either 6:30, half past six, or 6.30. English often says half past / quarter past / quarter to — gloss briefly if needed.
  • "Thirteen" vs "thirty", "forty-seven" vs "seventy-four". These confuse beginners. If missed, re-read slowly stressing the ending. Accept the digits (47) or the words.
  • Spelled-out letters. A Portuguese speaker may expect Portuguese letter names. If they mishear a letter, just re-read that letter clearly once. Accept the correct written letters even if order of guessing was bumpy.
  • Dates. "The third of June" = 3 June / 3rd June / June 3. Accept any clear written form. Do not require a specific format today.
  • Price/currency. Accept 5 dollars, $5, or five dollars.
  • Always correct gently and silently in writing — point, re-read once, let them fix it. No pressure.

Homework

Set the at-home listening from ielts/accent-listening.md — gentle, short listening to get the ear used to natural English and different accents. Remind the student: just listen for the key facts (names, numbers, times, places), not every word.

On the student sheet there is also a small written homework task: a short text for the student to read on their own (silently) and underline/write the key facts, so the "find the key word" habit continues between lessons. Bring the completed sheet next time.
