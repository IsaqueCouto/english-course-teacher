Lesson 1 — Present Simple: Daily Routines

How to teach this

This is the first lesson of the course. The student already knows the basics (greetings, numbers, "to be", everyday objects, family) and reads and writes simple sentences well (high A1). Present simple for daily routines is a natural next step: it lets him describe his real life in writing.

Keep the lesson gentle and scaffolded. The student does NOT speak in this lesson — every student task is reading, listening, recognition, or WRITING. You do all the talking and explaining; speaking is coached separately. When you want him to produce something, he writes it.

Use light Portuguese support: give a short PT gloss when you introduce a new grammar rule or a new instruction, but do not translate every word. He understands more than he can produce, so let him work it out in English first and only confirm in Portuguese.

Dictation is allowed: you say a short full sentence, he writes it. Never ask him to say, repeat, or read anything aloud.

Objective

By the end, the student can WRITE present-simple sentences (affirmative and negative) about daily routines, with correct word order, time expressions, and basic frequency adverbs, using I/you/we/they.

Target language

  • Routine verbs (~12): wake up, get up, have breakfast, have lunch, have dinner, go to work, start work, finish work, come home, study, watch TV, go to bed
  • Time expressions: at 7:00, in the morning, in the afternoon, in the evening, at night, every day
  • Frequency adverbs: always, usually, sometimes, never — placed BEFORE the verb (I always wake up early.)
  • Negative: don't + verb (I don't work on Sunday.)
  • Subjects: I / you / we / they only. (he / she / it comes in Lesson 2.)

Flow with timings (~45–50 min)

Warm-up (~5 min)

  • Greet the student and settle in. On the board or paper, write three short sentences about YOUR day: "I wake up at 6:00. I have breakfast in the morning. I go to bed at night."
  • Underline the time expressions. Ask the student (in writing) to copy the three sentences and underline the time words himself. PT: "Sublinhe as horas e os momentos do dia." This warms him up and previews the pattern with no pressure.

Present (~12 min)

  • Present the 12 routine verbs in a simple list. Mime or point to a clock/picture to make meaning clear — do not ask him to repeat. Give a one-word PT gloss only where needed (wake up = acordar, get up = levantar-se, have lunch = almoçar, go to bed = ir para a cama).
  • Teach the core rule on the board: Subject + verb + (time). "I work at night." Explain in English, then a short PT gloss: "Com I/you/we/they o verbo não muda."
  • Teach the negative: don't + verb. "I don't work at night." PT: "don't = não (forma negativa)."
  • Teach frequency adverbs and their position: always / usually / sometimes / never come BEFORE the main verb. Write: "I usually get up at 7:00." / "I never work on Sunday." PT: "always, usually, sometimes, never vêm ANTES do verbo."
  • Dictation check (writing): say 3 short sentences slowly, student writes them — "I get up at 7:00." / "We have lunch at noon." / "They don't watch TV." Then show him the correct versions so he can self-check.

Practice (~18 min)

  • Student works through the student-sheet exercises (a)–(e) in writing. Each exercise begins with a solved example and includes a word bank or hints.
  • Circulate and point silently at anything to reconsider; let him fix it in writing. Confirm a tricky point in PT if he is stuck, then return to English.
  • Watch especially for word order with frequency adverbs and for forgetting "don't" in negatives.

Produce — student WRITES ~4–5 sentences (~8 min)

  • Direct the student to exercise (f) "Write your own routine." He writes 4–5 sentences about a typical day using the given sentence starters/frame. No speaking — this is written production.
  • Give him the frame and let him fill it in. If he finishes early, ask him to add one negative sentence with "don't."

Wrap-up (~5 min)

  • Collect his "write your own routine" paragraph and read it back silently to yourself; mark only clear errors gently.
  • Recap the three rules on the board: (1) I/you/we/they + verb, (2) don't + verb for negative, (3) frequency adverb before the verb. Confirm each with a short PT gloss.
  • Assign homework.

Common mistakes & gentle corrections

  • Adding -s with I/you/we/they ("I works"). The -s form is Lesson 2. Gently: "With I, the verb stays plain — I work." PT: "Com I, o verbo fica simples."
  • Wrong frequency-adverb position ("I get up always early"). Show the correct order: "I always get up early." PT: "O advérbio vem antes do verbo."
  • Forgetting don't in negatives ("I no work" / "I not work"). Show: "I don't work." PT: "Use don't para o negativo."
  • Wrong preposition with time ("in 7:00" / "at the morning"). Remind: at + clock time, in + morning/afternoon/evening, at night. PT: "at para horas, in para parte do dia, at night."

Homework

Mirrors the student sheet:

  1. Finish any Practice items not completed in class.
  2. Write a short paragraph (4–5 sentences) about a family member's typical day using I/you/we/they style sentences (use "they" for the family member's activities, e.g. "They get up at 8:00.").
  3. Write 3 negative sentences with "don't" about things he does NOT do on weekends.
