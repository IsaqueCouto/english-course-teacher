Lesson 3 — Questions: Yes/No and Wh-

How to teach this

You do all the talking and explaining. The student only reads, listens, and writes — never asks the student to speak. When the plan says "dictation," you read the sentence aloud (twice, slowly) and the student writes it down. Keep the Portuguese support light: give a one-line PT gloss for each new rule, then continue in simple English. Praise correct word order before correcting small mistakes.

Objective

By the end, the student can read and write present-simple questions — yes/no questions with do/does and Wh- questions with what / where / when / who / how / why — and can write short answers (Yes, I do. / No, he doesn't.).

Target language

  • Question words: what / where / when / who / how / why
  • Yes/no questions: Do + I/you/we/they + base verb? / Does + he/she/it + base verb?
  • Short answers: Yes, I do. / No, I don't. / Yes, she does. / No, he doesn't.
  • Word order for Wh- questions: question word + do/does + subject + base verb?
  • Usage trap: after do/does, the main verb is always the base form (no -s).

Flow with timings (~45–50 min)

1. Warm-up (5 min)

Write three present-simple statements on the board that the student already knows the structure of: You work in an office. / She lives in a city. / They like coffee. Tell the student (in English, with a quick PT gloss) that today these statements will become questions. Do not ask the student to speak — just point and read each one aloud yourself so they connect sound to text.

2. Present (15 min)

Build the rules on the board, one at a time. Keep each PT gloss to one line.

  1. Yes/no questions. Take You work here. and show it becoming Do you work here? Explain that English adds a helper word do (or does) at the front. PT gloss: "O português não tem o auxiliar do/does — em inglês a pergunta precisa de do/does no início."
  2. do vs does. Do goes with I / you / we / they; does goes with he / she / it. Write the two columns.
  3. The base-verb trap. This is the most important point. After do/does, the main verb has no -s. Write the contrast clearly:
    • Does he work? ✓ / Does he works? ✗ PT gloss: "Depois de do/does, o verbo fica na forma base, sem -s. O does já carrega o -s."
  4. Wh- questions. Put the question word in front of the yes/no question: Where do you work? / What does she like? / When does the class start? Write the order on the board: question word + do/does + subject + base verb?
  5. Short answers. Do you work here? — Yes, I do. / No, I don't. and Does she live here? — Yes, she does. / No, she doesn't. Show that the short answer reuses do/does, not the main verb.

3. Practice (15 min)

The student works through Practice exercises (a)–(d) on the student sheet in writing. Sit beside them. For exercise (b) point to the word bank if they hesitate. Include a short dictation: read these two questions aloud slowly, twice each, and the student writes them — Where do you live? / Does she work on Monday? Then show the correct spelling so they can self-check.

4. Produce (10 min) [student WRITES]

The student writes original questions in exercises (e) and (f). For (e) they read the short paragraph and write 3–4 questions they could ask about that person. For (f) they use the sentence starters to write 4–5 questions for a new coworker. Encourage variety: at least one yes/no question and at least two Wh- questions. Do not ask them to speak the questions — collecting them in writing is the goal.

5. Wrap-up (5 min)

Read the student's produced questions aloud yourself and give quick written ticks/corrections. Re-state the one rule to remember: after do/does, the verb has no -s. Assign homework.

Common mistakes & gentle corrections

  • Keeping the -s after does: Does she works? → Gently underline works, write work above it, and remind: "the does already has the -s."
  • Forgetting do/does entirely: Where you live? → Add do: Where do you live? PT gloss: "em inglês a pergunta precisa do auxiliar."
  • Wrong helper: Does they like it? → Do they like it? Point back to the do/does columns.
  • Short answer reuses the main verb: Yes, I work. as an answer to Do you work? → Show Yes, I do. Praise the meaning, fix the form.

Homework

Set the Homework section on the student sheet: rewrite four statements as yes/no questions, complete four Wh- questions with the right question word, and write three short answers. Remind them in one line that the verb after do/does stays in the base form.
