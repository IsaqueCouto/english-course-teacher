Lesson 13 — The Future: "going to" and "will"

How to teach this

This is a writing and reading lesson. The student does not speak at any point. Every task is reading, listening while you read aloud, recognition, or writing. When you "present" language, write it on paper or a board and have the student read it silently, then copy it. Keep the two future forms close together so the student sees the contrast: going to for plans already decided, will for decisions made now, predictions, and offers. Use light Portuguese only to unlock meaning, not to translate everything. Go slowly and reward correct written sentences.

Objective

By the end, the student can write future sentences with be going to (plans and intentions) and will (decisions, predictions, offers), and can explain in a simple way which one to choose.

Target language

  • be going to + base verb (plans/intentions already decided): "I am going to study tonight." / "They are going to move next year."
  • will + base verb (decisions made now, predictions, offers): "It will rain tomorrow." / "I**'ll** help you."
  • Negatives: isn't/aren't going to ("She isn't going to work Sunday."), won't = will not ("I won't be late.").
  • Future time markers: tomorrow, next week, next year, soon, tonight.
  • Simple rule of thumb: going to = it was already planned; will = I decide it right now, or I predict it, or I offer something.
  • Usage trap: will + base verb, no "to" ("I will help", not "I will to help"); going to needs "be" ("She is going to...", not "She going to...").

Flow with timings (~45–50 min)

Warm-up (5 min). Write three sentences on paper: "I am going to cook dinner.", "It will rain.", "I'll help you." Ask the student to read them silently and, in writing, mark each one P (plan), Pr (prediction), or O (offer). Confirm answers together. PT gloss as needed: going to = plano já decidido; will = decisão agora / previsão / oferta.

Present (12 min). On paper, build the two patterns side by side:

  • going to: am/is/are + going to + base verb. Show the subject pairs (I am, he/she/it is, we/you/they are).
  • will: will + base verb (same for every subject). Show the short form 'll and the negative won't. Then show the negatives for going to (isn't/aren't going to). Have the student copy both patterns and underline the base verb in each. Point to the trap: no "to" after will; "be" is required before "going to".

Practice (15 min). Student completes exercises (a)–(d) on the student sheet in writing. Each exercise begins with a solved example. Circulate and check. For exercise (b), make sure the student can name (in writing, P or W) why each choice fits.

Produce — student WRITES (10 min). Student does exercise (e) (read the short text, answer in writing) and exercise (f) (write 4–5 sentences about their own plans for next week plus one prediction, using the sentence starters). This is the main writing output. Encourage real, true plans.

Wrap-up (5 min). Read the student's exercise (f) sentences aloud yourself while the student follows. Give gentle written corrections. Recap the simple rule one more time in a single written line: going to = já planejado; will = decisão agora / previsão.

Common mistakes & gentle corrections

  • "I will to help." → "I will help." (No "to" after will.) PT: depois de will vem o verbo base, sem to.
  • "She going to work." → "She is going to work." (Going to needs the verb "be".) PT: going to precisa do verbo be (am/is/are).
  • "He will helps me." → "He will help me." (Base verb after will, no -s.) PT: depois de will, sempre verbo base.
  • "I am going to studied." → "I am going to study." (Base verb after going to.) PT: depois de going to, verbo base.
  • Wrong choice (using will for a clear, already-made plan, e.g. a ticket is bought): gently point out the plan was already decided, so going to fits better.

Homework

On the student sheet: rewrite four sentences into the negative form, and write five short sentences about plans for the weekend (four "going to" plans + one "will" prediction) using the sentence starters. Bring the written work to the next lesson.
